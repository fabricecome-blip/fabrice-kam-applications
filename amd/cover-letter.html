Application · Senior Commercial Channel Account Manager · AMD To the AMD Commercial Sales Hiring Team

I have spent twenty years managing key accounts in consumer electronics across France and Southern Europe. What I really learned along the way is how to build channels that don't exist yet. At Acer, a PC company, I was part of a team that developed a Smartphone Business Unit from scratch across four Southern European markets simultaneously, drawing on the group's cross-functional support (finance, supply chain, marketing) to own the commercial side end to end. There was no playbook, no operator relationship and no internal precedent to build on. We earned credibility inside the organization while building the channels, signing the first operator agreements and activating over 10,000 points of sale across distribution, GMS and e-tail. The result was €50M in revenue and two million smartphones sold. That experience taught me that market share is won in partner enablement, buyer negotiations and pipeline execution rather than in strategy decks.

AMD is gaining ground fast in commercial computing. Client CPU unit share reached 29.6% in Q1 2026, up from 29.2% the prior quarter. Desktop share jumped to roughly 36%, up nearly 10 points year-on-year. Mobile share climbed to 26%, from 23.8% a year earlier. Intel still holds the larger base, but is openly struggling to respond before late 2026. In that window, channel account management makes the difference, because distributor programs and VAR and OEM enablement decide how much of this momentum converts into shelf space and design wins. This is the work I have been doing for twenty years, in a different category but with the same mechanics.

What I bring is ground-level command of B2B distribution mechanics. I've managed key account relationships with Orange, SFR, Bouygues, Fnac-Darty, Boulanger and Carrefour, negotiated multi-year framework agreements, and run a €50M P&L across four countries with weekly KPI discipline. All of it in practice, with an open P&L and C-level counterparts. I've also managed KAM and Trade Marketing teams, which taught me how to keep partner-facing teams aligned with strategy rather than the other way around.

What draws me to AMD is precisely this challenger momentum. AMD is no longer the alternative option, it's taking share quarter over quarter in a market Intel has held for decades. That shift happens largely through the channel, in the distributor's stock decisions, in the VAR's pitch and in the OEM's design-in conversations. I know that territory well, and I find it more motivating than defending an already-won position.

I also want to explain the 2019-2026 period rather than leave it unclear. I chose to step outside the KAM frame to build differently. D-Wager taught me what it means to found a brand and take it international with no commercial safety net. GreenB put me in front of a business under maximum operational pressure, leading insolvency proceedings for a €10M company. PCG and NextinCom brought me back to the fundamentals of pipeline, CRM discipline and recurring revenue. Those years gave me a broader view of what a good channel account manager needs to understand beyond his partner list.

I'll be honest about what I'm still building. I do not yet have deep knowledge of AMD's commercial PC, workstation and server portfolio, and closing that gap will be my first priority from day one. From the start I bring P&L discipline, a proven ability to build channel relationships from zero, and the habit of working cross-functionally with Sales, Marketing and Product Management so that what happens with partners stays consistent with what gets decided internally.

After a first conversation, I commit to delivering a priority partner analysis, a channel growth opportunity map, and a 30/60/90-day action plan, not as a proof of effort but as a starting point for the real work.
